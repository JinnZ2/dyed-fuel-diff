## Claims that must remain conditional

1. **D1–D6 cannot establish dye causation on their own.** Co-onset after fueling, cross-unit co-occurrence or recovery after a different tank may implicate a shared fuel supply, storage contamination, additives, a different base fuel, or another common cause. A changed tank changes several variables simultaneously. Even D5's visible leak establishes a possible physical path, not which chemical caused it. D7's assay confirms only dye presence—not the mechanism. That overclaim in `04_discriminating_checks.md` was CORRECTED in the 2026-10-02 patch (P1): D1–D6 now read as discriminating a shared fuel-batch cause from independent faults, not isolating the dye.
2. **Actual sensor technology is unknown.** The matrix proposes an optical/turbidity WIF effect, while at least one [commercial WIF design is capacitive](https://rochestersensors.com/product/capacitive-water-in-fuel-sensors/). Presence, architecture, signal processing and control authority of all eleven listed sensors must be checked for a specified vehicle before treating the rows as an equipment inventory. Row 2 is now split into 2a optical / 2b capacitive (P2).
3. **Ladders and arbitration are not OEM evidence.** There is no supplied controller log, calibration, model-year or service manual establishing C1–C5, automatic safe-stop, injector cutout, or the precise chain from two flags to five-mph limp. [EPA's DEF/SCR guidance](https://www.epa.gov/regulations-emissions-vehicles-and-engines/diesel-exhaust-fluid) addresses some severe inducements but does not prove the proposed NOx+DPF combination or a universal regulatory response. “High confidence” in `02_triggering_actions.md` is the proposed failure mode, **not a measured controller state**.
4. **The proportionality verdicts are conditional rankings.** A small *assumed* signal offset versus a large *assumed* action might be disproportionate, but neither the deviation nor the OEM action has been observed here. A true water/rail-pressure problem could warrant a strong response. Record the actual condition and road context before ranking actions.
5. **Materials results are exposure-specific.** Published Jatropha/diesel and other blend studies demonstrate methods for measuring seal changes; they do not implicate the red marker or its carrier in any fitted seal. The user's inline literature split's simplified “FKM/Viton holds, others swell/crack” is not a general result of the [cited experiment](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.renene.2018.04.048); results vary with material and fuel. 03 now cites the study as method precedent only (P3).
6. The pre-1990 / newer split is an OBSERVED fleet decision by one operation, with OBSERVED seal deterioration and heat acceleration — scope: that operation. Generalizing it requires the inventory and bench run. Whether every listed sensor feeds control is unverified per vehicle. Red color alone does not identify sulfur grade.

## Responsible next experiment

Use an independently characterized **matched fuel batch** and separate base fuel, dye, commercial carrier/package, and contaminated-storage conditions. Bench-test the **actual named** sensors, elastomers and fault logic at recorded temperatures. Pre-register measurements, comparators, failure thresholds and stop criteria. Observe whether a component measures a difference *before* attributing any vehicle action to it.

**What was done for this repo:** source reading and editorial review only. **What was not done:** fuel assay, seal immersion, sensor bench test, fleet analysis, software fault injection or an OEM action-map verification.
