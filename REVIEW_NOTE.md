# Review note — how to read this draft

**Status: editorial boundary, not a fourth observation.** The three user-supplied files `01_matrix.md`, `02_triggering_actions.md`, and `04_discriminating_checks.md` were preserved byte-for-byte. Their statements are **hypotheses and candidate test outcomes**; none identifies a vehicle configuration or records a controlled dyed-versus-undyed result. [`03_literature.md`](03_literature.md) checks and cites adjacent sources, but the proposed causal join remains **UNRUN**.

## Claims that must remain conditional

1. **D1–D6 cannot establish dye causation on their own.** Co-onset after fueling, cross-unit co-occurrence or recovery after a different tank may implicate a shared fuel supply, storage contamination, additives, a different base fuel, or another common cause. A changed tank changes several variables simultaneously. Even D5's visible leak establishes a possible physical path, not which chemical caused it. D7's assay confirms only dye presence—not the mechanism. The phrase “D1–D6 establish that the dye is the cause” in the submitted `04_discriminating_checks.md` is **not an established inference**.
2. **Actual sensor technology is unknown.** The matrix proposes an optical/turbidity WIF effect, while at least one [commercial WIF design is capacitive](https://rochestersensors.com/product/capacitive-water-in-fuel-sensors/). Presence, architecture, signal processing and control authority of all eleven listed sensors must be checked for a specified vehicle before treating the rows as an equipment inventory.
3. **Ladders and arbitration are not OEM evidence.** There is no supplied controller log, calibration, model-year or service manual establishing C1–C5, automatic safe-stop, injector cutout, or the precise chain from two flags to five-mph limp. [EPA's DEF/SCR guidance](https://www.epa.gov/regulations-emissions-vehicles-and-engines/diesel-exhaust-fluid) addresses some severe inducements but does not prove the proposed NOx+DPF combination or a universal regulatory response. “High confidence” in `02_triggering_actions.md` is the proposed failure mode, **not a measured controller state**.
4. **The proportionality verdicts are conditional rankings.** A small *assumed* signal offset versus a large *assumed* action might be disproportionate, but neither the deviation nor the OEM action has been observed here. A true water/rail-pressure problem could warrant a strong response. Record the actual condition and road context before ranking actions.
5. **Materials results are exposure-specific.** Published Jatropha/diesel and other blend studies demonstrate methods for measuring seal changes; they do not implicate the red marker or its carrier in any fitted seal. The user's inline literature split's simplified “FKM/Viton holds, others swell/crack” is not a general result of the [cited experiment](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.renene.2018.04.048); results vary with material and fuel.
6. **No age cutoff or “all fuel-coupled sensors feed CONTROL” has been verified.** The purported pre-1990/newer split and every row's control coupling should be separately inventoried and tested. [EPA describes ULSD requirements for on-road and nonroad fuel](https://www.epa.gov/diesel-fuel-standards/diesel-fuel-standards-and-rulemakings); red color by itself does not identify higher sulfur or a different fuel grade.

## Responsible next experiment

Use an independently characterized **matched fuel batch** and separate base fuel, dye, commercial carrier/package, and contaminated-storage conditions. Bench-test the **actual named** sensors, elastomers and fault logic at recorded temperatures. Pre-register measurements, comparators, failure thresholds and stop criteria. Observe whether a component measures a difference *before* attributing any vehicle action to it. In field diagnostics, use authorized qualified personnel and normal manufacturer service procedures; do **not** use tax-exempt dyed fuel on public roads as a trial or override emissions/safety systems. [IRS inspection guidance](https://www.irs.gov/irm/part4/irm_04-024-015r) describes dyed-fuel enforcement in on-road vehicles.

**What was done for this repo:** source reading and editorial review only. **What was not done:** fuel assay, seal immersion, sensor bench test, fleet analysis, software fault injection or an OEM action-map verification.
